Vienbi

VNB · the von Neumann–Bernays Math Assistant

Vienbi (written VNB) is an interactive proof assistant — a program you do mathematics with. You state a theorem and build its proof a step at a time; the machine checks every step against a small, auditable logical kernel, so a finished proof is correct by construction, not by trust.

Its foundation is a first-order set theory in the style of von Neumann–Bernays: one universe of classes, with membership (∈) the only primitive notion. But you rarely touch that bedrock — numbers, functions, and ordinals are built in as primitives governed by ordinary axioms, so a proof reads the way mathematics is actually written, not as a tower of encodings.

It is also a small experiment: a rigorous proof system built largely by an AI, with mathematical direction from F. J. Thayer.

How it fits together

The engine

An MIT Scheme program: the logical kernel, the library of structures and theorems, and the tactics you steer a proof with. The part that does the checking.

This page

Where you read: browse the library, the catalog of theorems, and the clickable structure graph. No setup — just links in your browser.

Emacs

Where you work: starting or continuing a proof opens an Emacs buffer. The launcher configures it for you; it’s the worktable where steps get typed.

New to Emacs? You don’t need to learn it. The launcher sets everything up, and the buttons below open the right window on their own. In practice you type your goal and press a single chord (C-c C-c) to send it to the prover. Think of Emacs here as the table you work at, not a thing to study — the mathematics is the point.

How results are classified
Axiom
A primitive of VNB. The base we build on — small and essentially fixed.
Definition
True by construction (a defining equation, or an unfolding of one). Not a theorem, so it is never proved and is not a Proof Support Set member — but it still fires as a rewrite in proofs.
Theorem
Carries a machine proof, re-certified by the VNB test.
Proof Support Set (PSS)
A theorem we trust on a warrant, without a machine proof — excused from the VNB test. Each entry carries credentials: a plain statement plus why we accept it.

Firing is a separate matter. Whether the prover may apply a result as a rewrite is independent of this classification: axioms, definitions, theorems, and PSS members can all be rewrite rules. A definition keeps that right even though it is not a PSS member.
