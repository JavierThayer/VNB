\section{The experiment}

\begin{frame}{This is a project in progress}
  \fid{in-progress}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Not a released system. No users besides the speaker.
    \item The talk reports a \emph{state}: what is proven, what is asserted
          without proof, what is broken, and what was found broken last month.
    \item Every number quoted is dated. Today's are as of \today.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \begin{block}{The unit of progress}
    Not ``one more theorem''. The deliverable of a proof attempt here is
    usually the obstacle it exposes --- and twice this summer the obstacle was
    a soundness bug in the checker.
  \end{block}
  \vfill
  And when a proof turned into a \emph{grind} for the model, that was a finding
  too: it pointed at a missing tactic, or at foundational work not yet done ---
  not at a missing lemma. One general mechanism that dissolves a class of
  obligations beats $N$ lemmas that discharge them one at a time.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What language models are currently claimed to do in mathematics}
  \fid{llm-claims}
  A partial list of the claims, in the claimants' own framing:
  \vspace{1ex}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Competition problems.} AlphaGeometry (2024) on olympiad
          geometry; AlphaProof (2024) reported at IMO silver-medal level,
          producing Lean proofs; in 2025, gold-medal-level scores reported for
          general models reasoning in natural language.
    \item \textbf{Assistance inside a proof assistant.} Premise selection,
          tactic suggestion and autoformalisation for Lean/mathlib
          (LeanCopilot, LLMStep, draft-sketch-prove pipelines).
    \item \textbf{Large collaborative formalisation.} The Equational Theories
          Project and similar efforts, where models handle routine cases at
          scale under human direction.
    \item \textbf{Search and discovery.} LLM-guided evolutionary search
          (AlphaEvolve, 2025) reported to improve constructions and bounds on
          open combinatorial problems; assisted literature search closing
          problems listed as open.
    \item \textbf{Benchmarks.} FrontierMath, PutnamBench and the like, with
          scores climbing.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What those projects are, and what they are not}
  \fid{llm-assessment}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Every project on the previous slide uses a language model to
          \emph{tackle challenging mathematical problems}.
    \item None of them is a general tool for formalising proofs --- possibly
          interactively --- that works \emph{independently of a language
          model}.
    \item That is a different object, and it is the one this project is
          building.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \begin{block}{The distinction, in one line}
    Their subject is what the model can solve. Ours is what the tool can
    check, and who or what can drive it.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A goal: proof construction with minimal intervention}
  \fid{minimal-intervention}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Speculatively, without a model at all.} A user supplies
          a \emph{preamble} --- the definitions, the statement, the facts to be
          used --- and a formal proof is constructed from it without any
          intervention by a language model.
    \item[(b)] \textbf{By a language model} that drives the existing tactics to
          construct a formal proof.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  Both are open, and \textbf{(a) is the major line of subsequent work} --- it
  asks how much of a proof is already determined by a well-stated preamble.
  \vfill
  \begin{block}{Where that would be worth having}
    Mathematics education, and other settings where a formal model is wanted
    to pin down reasoning that prose leaves loose.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What a preamble would have to contain}
  \fid{preamble-programme}
  Speculative, and stated so --- but this is where the project goes next, so it
  is worth being precise about what is being claimed.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The claim.} For a large class of ordinary results, the proof
          is nearly determined once the statement, the definitions it unfolds,
          and the facts a mathematician would cite are all written down. The
          search that remains is small enough for a machine without a model in
          the loop.
    \item \textbf{Why it is not absurd here.} The pieces exist already: named
          facts that transport as rewrites; a hierarchy that discharges
          subsumption without being asked; oracles that end arithmetic and
          order goals outright; a library of statements-without-proofs to draw
          citations from; and tactics that are ordinary programs, so a search
          composes them rather than generating text.
    \item \textbf{What would count as evidence.} A preamble written for a
          theorem the author has \emph{not} proved by hand, from which the
          machine closes it. Not yet attempted.
    \item \textbf{What would count against it.} Preambles that turn out to be
          proofs in disguise --- if writing one costs what writing the proof
          costs, the claim is empty.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A different question}
  \fid{the-question}
  \begin{center}
    \Large
    Not: \emph{can a language model do mathematics?}\\[2ex]
    But: \emph{can a language model build the instrument?}
  \end{center}
  \vfill
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The instrument here is a proof checker with a small trusted kernel,
          a structure library, a tactic layer and its own documentation.
    \item The experiment ran for months of daily sessions, and the artifact is
          on disk.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Why a proof checker is the right test object}
  \fid{why-a-checker}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Its output is checkable \emph{without trusting the author's
          fluency}. A proof passes the kernel or it does not.
    \item Failure is visible and diagnosable: an open leaf, a rejected rule
          application, a bill that names an assumption nobody justified.
    \item Contrast: a convincing English proof produced by a model is exactly
          as convincing when it is wrong.
    \item The bet is on the artifact being inspectable --- not on the model
          being right.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \small
  \emph{The honest counterweight, and it is the subject of the last part of
  this talk: the kernel that does the checking was written by the same model.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Lineage: IMPS}
  \fid{imps-lineage}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Not built from nothing. The design descends from \imps{}, by
          William Farmer, Joshua Guttman and Javier Thayer --- the speaker's
          own prior work, so: framed here, not explained.
    \item Carried over: \emph{macetes} (named rewriting/transportation of
          facts), the deduction graph, and partial equality with explicit
          definedness.
    \item \emph{Macete} is Portuguese: a clever trick, very nearly a sleight of
          hand. The word is doing real work --- it names a fact repackaged as a
          move, rather than a fact.
    \item Not carried over: the LUTINS type system and the little-theories
          machinery with its interpretations; \vnb{} is one theory over
          von Neumann--Bernays set theory, with structures as tuples.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Why Scheme}
  \fid{why-scheme}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A formula \emph{is} the host language's own data type. No parser
          between the checker and its subject matter, and a tactic that builds
          a formula is ordinary code --- which is the point of the syntax
          slides later on.
    \item Macros let the surface grow: \code{declare-structure} and the
          backward-chaining form are notation, not library calls.
    \item The read-eval-print loop \emph{is} the programmatic surface. There
          was no interface to design before there was one to use.
    \item MIT Scheme compiles to native code, and it matters more than it
          should: the library re-proves itself in \Nload{} seconds compiled and
          about eleven minutes interpreted.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \begin{block}{The lineage, and the caution}
    \imps{} was written in T --- a Scheme dialect, and now defunct. Choosing a
    small language with one good implementation is a bet on that
    implementation; the previous bet lost. Nothing here defends against that
    except keeping the trusted part small enough to move.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

\section{The logic}

\begin{frame}{Why von Neumann--Bernays}
  \fid{why-nbg}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Classes are objects of the language. ``The class of all groups'' is
          sayable without a metalanguage and without universe levels.
    \item Sets are exactly the classes that can be members. The distinction is
          a predicate, $x \in \Set$, not a syntactic layer.
    \item In practice, for a structure library: a structure is a tuple, a
          carrier is a set, and a construction that would leave the universe
          (the class of all ordinals, a function space over a proper class)
          fails by owing a \emph{sethood obligation} rather than by being
          unsayable.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \small The price: sethood obligations are real proof work, and one of the
  soundness bugs on a later slide is exactly a rule that forgot to demand one.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Equality is partial}
  \fid{partial-equality}
  This is the single largest departure from what an audience expecting HOL will
  assume.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Terms may be undefined: $\operatorname{pred}(0)$,
          $\operatorname{recip}(0)$, a $\lambda$ applied outside its domain.
    \item $t = t$ is therefore \emph{not} a tautology: it asserts that $t$ is
          defined.
    \item $s == t$ (quasi-equality) is the weaker statement: both undefined,
          or both defined and equal.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \begin{block}{Consequence in the kernel}
    Reflexivity carries a definedness guard. Deleting that guard is one of the
    five mutations in the last part of this talk --- and it makes
    $\operatorname{pred}(0) = \operatorname{pred}(0)$ provable.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The language}
  \fid{the-language}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Terms and formulas, first order, with class terms.
    \item Guarded quantification is primitive notation:
          \code{forall([x in A], p)} for
          $\forall x.\, x \in A \Rightarrow p$, and dually for
          \code{forsome}.
    \item Definite description \code{IOTA}: \code{iota(y, p)} is the unique
          $y$ satisfying $p$, undefined when there is none or many. This is how
          \code{pred} is defined, and why it is undefined at $0$.
    \item $\lambda$ carries its domain: \code{VNB-LAMBDA(x, A, b)} is defined
          on $A$ only. Applying it elsewhere is undefined, not an error.
    \item Application is defined by the graph:
          $f(x) == \operatorname{iota} y.\ \langle x,y\rangle \in f$.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \small A formula is an s-expression, and that is not an implementation
  detail: it is the next three slides.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Formulas are data, and are built by program}
  \fid{formulas-are-data}
  \code{IS-DENSE-SEQ}, as it is actually defined --- note the alternation
  $\forall x\, \forall \varepsilon\, \exists m$:
  \codefile{dense-seq.txt}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item No nested \code{AND}/\code{IMPLIES} pyramid is written by hand. The
          guards are a flat list; the constructor does the nesting.
    \item The reason is empirical: a hand-counted pyramid loses its last close
          paren, and the whole library load dies. Three times in one session.
    \item A \emph{formula} proper is a record wrapping one of these s-expressions
          thinly --- the expression plus the theory it was formed in.
          \code{(wff-formula w)} extracts the s-expression; everything that
          walks or rewrites a formula works on that.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{The constructor, and where it came from}
  \fid{forall-guarded}
  \codefile{forall-guarded.txt}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item \code{(forall-guarded '(u\_ v\_) '((IN u\_ RR) (IN v\_ RR)) body)}
          $\;=\;$ $\forall u,v.\ u \in \mathbb{R} \Rightarrow v \in \mathbb{R}
          \Rightarrow \textit{body}$ --- the shape almost every lemma has.
    \item A bare symbol instead of a list means one binder, which is what an
          \emph{alternating} pyramid needs: peel one at a time and the
          quantifiers interleave in the order written.
    \item \textbf{This constructor was the Assistant's own proposal}, not the
          speaker's --- a response to a failure it kept causing. Seven files
          had independently grown private copies of the same two one-liners;
          they are now one pair, in one place.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{A proof pattern, parameterised by a formula}
  \fid{quasiquote}
  Excluded middle on an arbitrary $P$, as a reusable move --- the backquote
  builds the formula, the comma drops $P$ into it:
  \codefile[\tiny]{em-pattern.txt}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item \code{bm-cases} then takes any $P$ and hands back the case split.
    \item This is the point of the syntax being data: a search for a proof does
          not write formulas, it writes \emph{programs that write formulas},
          and Scheme's quasiquote is already the right notation for that.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The kernel}
  \fid{the-kernel}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \Nkernel{} primitive inference procedures
          (\code{primitive-inferences.scm}). Nothing else in the system may
          assert anything.
    \item Everything above them --- tactics, macetes, drivers, the oracles ---
          is \emph{untrusted}, and works only by reducing to those \Nkernel.
    \item Some principles are rules rather than axioms because they carry a
          schema over formulas: separation, comprehension, indexed union.
    \item \Nunexer{} documented rule tags are not exercised by any proof in the
          current load. An unexercised rule is where unsoundness hides; that
          list is a worklist.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Eigenvariables}
  \fid{eigenvariables}
  The one piece of proof-theoretic vocabulary the rest of this talk assumes.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item To prove $\forall x.\, P(x)$, introduce a fresh name $a$ and prove
          $P(a)$. To use a hypothesis $\exists x.\, P(x)$, introduce a fresh
          name $a$ and assume $P(a)$.
    \item That fresh name is the \emph{eigenvariable}. It stands for an
          arbitrary object in the first case and for an unknown witness in the
          second, and in both it is opaque: nothing may be assumed about it
          beyond what was written down.
    \item The rule's \emph{side condition} is what makes this sound: the
          eigenvariable must not already occur free --- not in the goal, not in
          any hypothesis. The kernel checks that; it is the reason these rules
          are procedures and not axioms.
    \item So the machine must invent names, and they reach the page: the
          \code{u\_}, \code{v\_}, \code{n\_} in the proofs shown on later
          slides are eigenvariables, carrying the trailing underscore of the
          naming rule on the case-folding slide.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Caveat: the system is non-constructive}
  \fid{non-constructive}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The logic is classical. Excluded middle is available for any
          formula, and proof by contradiction is a primitive move.
    \item Choice is available and used: Zorn's lemma is proven in the library,
          unconditionally relative to the kernel.
    \item Definite description (\code{IOTA}) names an object by a property it
          uniquely satisfies. That is a description, not a construction.
    \item Consequence, stated plainly: a proof that something \emph{exists}
          here need not hand you the thing. No extraction, no programs from
          proofs.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \small This is a deliberate choice --- the target is ordinary classical
  mathematics as it is written --- but it rules out a family of applications,
  and an audience that expects a constructive system should be told at the
  start.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{One kernel rule, in full}
  \fid{kernel-rule-source}
  \codefile[\tiny]{pi-reflexivity.txt}
  \vspace{-0.5ex}
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
    \item \textbf{What it does:} it closes the leaf \code{sqn} when the goal is
          \code{TRUTH}, or is $s = t$ with $s$ and $t$ alpha-equivalent
          \emph{and} that term known to be defined --- defined syntactically
          (a variable, a ground term, a tree of total operations), or witnessed
          by an assumption in the context.
    \item The \code{if} is the whole rule: either the side condition holds and
          the graph gets an edge, or the rule returns \code{\#f} and nothing
          happened --- no error, no half-applied step.
    \item That definedness conjunct is the partiality of $=$ made operational.
          Delete it and $\operatorname{pred}(0) = \operatorname{pred}(0)$
          becomes provable, which is one of the five planted mutations.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The deduction graph}
  \fid{deduction-graph}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A proof is not a term; it is a \emph{graph of sequent nodes}, built
          by navigation. Open leaves are the outstanding obligations.
    \item A tactic moves focus and posts nodes; \code{qed} is legal exactly
          when no leaf is open.
    \item Nodes are hash-consed up to alpha-equivalence of the assertion, so
          the same obligation reached twice is the same node --- which is
          efficient, and which is also the source of a real trap (cutting a
          formula already in context yields a self-loop, not two leaves).
    \item This graph is what the whole user interface is about: the Emacs
          surface, the proof printer, and the natural-language reader all
          render it.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Two surfaces for composing a proof}
  \fid{two-surfaces}
  A proof is not a term, so it has to be \emph{navigated}. There are two ways
  to do that, logically equivalent and differing only in how arguments arrive.
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{(a) Interactive.} The Focus Workspace in Emacs: single-key
      commands, menus, arguments typed \emph{bare} at a prompt --- a lemma's
      name with no quote --- and the command assembles the call.
      \vspace{1ex}\\
      Influenced directly by the \imps{} user interface.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{(b) Programmatic.} The REPL and saved proof scripts: the tactic
      is written as a Scheme expression with the quoting explicit ---
      \code{(fact 'metric-sym)}, \code{(ai '(FORSOME rad \dots))}.
      \vspace{1ex}\\
      This is the surface an automated search wants, and the one a proof file
      is made of.
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vfill
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Everything the library contains was built on (b) --- a proof is a
          file, and re-running it is how the library is re-proved at every
          boot.
    \item The speaker nonetheless works through (a) on purpose, to keep the
          interactive surface honest. Most of its rough edges were found that
          way.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{When a proof grinds, build the mechanism}
  \fid{minimize-example}
  A worked instance of the previous claim --- that a grind names a missing
  tactic.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Two proofs needed ``choose the entry of least degree'', ``choose the
          pivot with the smallest value''. Both were stalling, and both were
          about to be asserted as textbook facts.
    \item Instead: one tactic. \code{(minimize! vars GUARD MEASURE)} ---
          \emph{choose \code{vars} satisfying GUARD with MEASURE as small as
          possible} --- leaving only the two obligations any minimisation owes:
          that MEASURE lands in $\mathbb{N}$, and that GUARD is satisfiable at
          all.
    \item Both stalled facts became theorems, and the next proof that needs a
          minimal anything pays nothing.
    \item It uses no choice: well-ordering returns a \emph{member} of the value
          set, and the value set is a separation set, so the witness is
          recoverable.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Structures}

\begin{frame}{A structure is a tuple}
  \fid{structure-what}
  This is the standard idiom, not an invention of the system. A measure space
  is a triple $(X, \mathcal{A}, \mu)$ with $\mathcal{A}$ a $\sigma$-algebra on
  $X$ and $\mu$ a countably additive measure on $\mathcal{A}$; a ring is
  $(A, +, \cdot, -, 0, 1)$. The idiom has minor variations, and it is widely
  accepted and understood.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item So: a structure is a \emph{tuple}, plus a predicate saying which
          tuples qualify. No new logical machinery --- rings are objects of the
          theory, and everything true of sets is available about them.
    \item \code{declare-structure} names the slots and generates one accessor
          per slot (\code{CARR}, \code{ADD}, \code{MUL}, \dots), each denoting
          exactly one position.
    \item \code{IS-RING(s)} is then a defined predicate: the right length, the
          carrier a set, each operation in the right function space, and the
          laws.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \small The variation the system does \emph{not} support is the one that
  leaves the tuple: no bundled proofs, no dependent record. What is bundled is
  data; what qualifies it is a formula.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{An aside: case folding}
  \fid{case-folding}
  A minor inconvenience, mentioned because its fingerprints are on the slides.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Both the \vnb{} reader and the host Scheme fold symbols to lower
          case. \code{X} \emph{is} \code{x}; \code{SP} is \code{sp}.
    \item Consequence 1 --- accessors avoid single letters. The carrier was
          \code{X} and is \code{CARR}; a metric space has \code{PTS} and
          \code{DIST}, not \code{X} and \code{D}.
    \item Consequence 2 --- an inner binder that would collide takes a trailing
          underscore: \code{i\_}, \code{n\_}, and the \code{u\_}, \code{v\_}
          in the proofs on later slides.
    \item Consequence 3 --- a definition in a proof script can silently rebind
          a tactic (\code{(define BC \dots)} kills the \code{bc} tactic). Two
          load-time audits and a per-file environment now catch that.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \small Cost: three naming rules and three audits. Not deep --- but it is the
  kind of thing an honest report of a working system includes.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Ring, as actual source (1/2)}
  \fid{ring-declare}
  \codefile{ring-declare.txt}
  \vspace{-0.5ex}
  \small
  One declaration produces the accessors, the sethood and function-space
  conjuncts, and the definition of \code{IS-RING} as an
  \emph{if and only if} --- which is why its projections cost nothing in the
  trust ledger, a later slide.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Ring, as actual source (2/2)}
  \fid{ring-axiom}
  \codefile{ring-axiom.txt}
  \vspace{-0.5ex}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Note the shape: guarded on \code{IS-RING}, then on membership in the
          carrier. Every fact about rings has this prefix, and peeling it is
          what the tactic layer spends its time on.
    \item Note also the honest defect: this one is installed as
          \code{asserted} with no warrant, though it is a projection of the
          definition above. See the trust-ledger slide.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The hierarchy}
  \fid{hierarchy}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item ``A Euclidean ring is a ring'' is \emph{recorded}, not re-proved at
          each use: each definitional structure registers its parent chain.
    \item The checker uses it to discharge subsumption steps automatically, and
          the documentation generator uses the same table to draw the structure
          graph.

\begin{frame}{A hard edge: abstract categories}
  \fid{categories}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The idiom on the previous slides wants a structure to be a
          \emph{tuple of sets}. An abstract category --- the objects being all
          groups, all spaces --- has no such carrier: it is a proper class.
    \item In NBG that class is perfectly sayable. What it cannot be is a
          \emph{member}, and so it cannot sit in a slot of a tuple. The
          machinery for structures does not reach it.
    \item \textbf{Small categories are fine}, and take the existing machinery
          unchanged: objects a set, arrows a set, composition an operation on
          them. Most of what one wants to prove --- functors, limits,
          adjunctions in a fixed small setting --- lives there.
    \item Large categories would need a second mechanism (a
          class-indexed structure, or a universe convention). Not built, and
          not currently planned.
  \end{itemize}
  %% ------------------------------------------------------------------
  %% BACK POCKET -- not for projection, for the question from the floor.
  %% "So you cannot do category theory."  Answer: given an ordinal, any
  %% structure relativises to the structures lying in V of that ordinal.  Pick
  %% it large enough to be closed under the constructions in play and the
  %% category of those groups/spaces is set-carried, taking the existing
  %% machinery unchanged; every concrete theorem one wants holds there.  So the
  %% small-category restriction is sometimes AWKWARD -- the ordinal is a
  %% parameter, it appears in the statements, and relating two choices of it is
  %% an argument nobody wants to write twice -- but it is not disastrous.
  %% Check before claiming it as available TODAY: the library has the ordinals
  %% and transfinite induction; whether the rank hierarchy V_alpha is actually
  %% defined has not been verified.  Until it is, the honest form is "the
  %% standard move is available in the logic", not "we do this".
  %% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\end{frame}

    \item One table keyed by head symbol, populated at definition time.
    \item Read by the English verbaliser, the glossary, the structure
          documentation and the proof reader. Notation is not re-implemented
          per output format.
    \item The same table is now the registry the substitution walker consults,

\begin{frame}{Design in progress: \code{FUBA-CLASS}}
  \fid{fuba-class}
  Candidate, not committed --- included because ``in progress'' should be
  shown, not asserted.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Today each structure \code{FUBA} comes with a predicate
          \code{IS-FUBA}.
    \item Proposal: also a class \code{FUBA-CLASS}, so that
          \[
            \forall x.\ \operatorname{IS-FUBA}(x) \Rightarrow \textsc{blah}
            \qquad\text{may be written}\qquad
            \forall x \in \operatorname{FUBA-CLASS}.\ \textsc{blah}
          \]

    \item The one condition to state: in NBG only \emph{sets} are members, so
          the equivalence needs \code{IS-FUBA(x)} to imply that $x$ is a set.
          Free here --- structures are tuples.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{What may honestly be claimed}

\begin{frame}{The claim, and its limits}
  \fid{soundness-claim}
  \begin{block}{Not claimable}
    ``The system is sound.'' The kernel is unverified Scheme, written by the
    same model, and it has been unsound twice in the last month.
  \end{block}
  \begin{block}{Claimable, and checkable}
    \vnb{} does not ask you to trust its proofs. It asks you to trust a small
    kernel --- and it tells you, per theorem, what \emph{else} it is trusting.
  \end{block}
  \vfill
  The slides that follow are that ledger, those bugs, and the apparatus that
  found them.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Warrants: saying why an unproved fact is believed}
  \fid{warrants}
  A library fact that is asserted rather than proved is \emph{debt}. A
  \code{warrant!} is the note attached to that debt saying what backs it.
  \vspace{1ex}
  \begin{center}\small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
    \toprule
    tier & the fact is backed by\dots \\
    \midrule
    \code{none}       & \textbf{nothing at all} --- asserted, never justified \\
    \code{hand-wave}  & a plausibility argument, not a rigorous one \\
    \code{well-known} & a standard textbook fact, asserted without argument \\
    \code{reference}  & a specific named source, cited \\
    \code{informal}   & a rigorous paper proof exists, just not mechanised \\
    \code{proof}      & a machine-checked \vnb{} proof exists \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{1ex}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item Weakest to strongest, in that order. \code{informal} outranks
          \code{reference}: a real proof nobody mechanised beats a citation
          nobody checked.
    \item A warrant is a \emph{claim}, not a proof. Its value is that it is
          written down, attached to the fact, and shows up in every bill that
          leans on it.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Every theorem carries a bill}
  \fid{bills}
  \codefile[\tiny]{bills.txt}
  \vspace{-0.5ex}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item An empty bill prints \code{proven modulo 0}: nothing asserted stands
          between the theorem and the kernel. That is the strongest report.
    \item A non-empty bill carries the warrant of its \emph{weakest} leaf.
          \code{trust: none} means some leaf justifies nothing at all.
    \item Of \Nproven{} proven theorems, \Nmodzero{} are unconditional;
          \Nbills{} carry debt, and \Ntrustnone{} of those are
          \code{trust: none} --- 54 the day before this deck was assembled.
    \item The tier is the \emph{weakest} leaf, which makes progress lumpy and
          citation counts misleading. Of the ten repairs that took it from 54
          to \Ntrustnone{}, \emph{three moved nothing at all}: each discharged
          a leaf that a second unwarranted leaf was shadowing in every bill
          naming it --- and each was still necessary, since that second leaf
          had to go too. Discharging a leaf pays only when it is the
          \emph{last} one.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Oracles: the other half of the trust surface}
  \fid{oracles}
  A warrant is debt the bill can name. An \emph{oracle} leaves nothing to name
  --- and that is the problem it took until 2026-08-05 to fix.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Six verbs close goals by \textbf{computation} rather than by kernel
          rules: \code{ineq} (Fourier--Motzkin/Farkas over linear order),
          \code{arith} (ground arithmetic), \code{rs} / \code{crs} /
          \code{simp} (ring normalisation), \code{sos} (sums of squares).
    \item Each is trusted \emph{code} --- sound on its domain, but believed
          rather than checked --- and each closes its leaf outright. So
    \begin{center}\code{;; qed rr-zero-lt-one: proven modulo 0}\end{center}
          read ``unconditional'' when the proof was one \code{ineq} call and
          nothing else.
    \item Now a bill carries them, transitively, exactly as it carries debt:
          \code{[oracles: arith ineq]}. A theorem citing an oracle-closed lemma
          inherits the oracle.
    \item Disclosed, not repaired. The repair for the largest one is known:
          \code{ineq} \emph{is} Farkas, so it already computes a certificate ---
          emit it, check it, and the oracle becomes checked computation.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Three real soundness bugs, all found this summer}
  \fid{soundness-bugs}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The $\lambda$-domain hole.} A typing rule certified
          \code{VNB-LAMBDA(x, x)} into $A \to A$ for \emph{every} $A$; with
          $A = \emptyset$ that proves $0 \in \emptyset$ --- \emph{modulo 0}, a
          false theorem with a clean bill. Fixed 2026-08-02.
    \item \textbf{Simultaneous substitution.} The rewriter folded a
          multi-variable substitution into iterated single substitutions, so
          one binding could rewrite inside the term another had just placed. No
          error, no warning: a different theorem. Fixed 2026-07-11.
    \item \textbf{Unregistered heads.} 71 applied operator heads were not in
          the registry the substitution walker consults, so they were read as
          applied \emph{variables}. Found by a new load-time sweep, 2026-08-04.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \small Each was found by strengthening the apparatus, not by a proof looking
  wrong.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{The must-not-prove corpus}
  \fid{must-not-prove}
  Every other test asks ``does this still work?''. These ask the opposite.
  \codefile[\scriptsize]{mnp-partiality.txt}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item \Nmnp{} false statements, pushed through the real tactic layer with
          the whole library loaded; each must leave the proof \emph{open}.
    \item \Nmnpctl{} \emph{paired controls}: the same machinery on a true
          statement must close. A control that stops closing means the attack
          went dead and the refusal was worthless.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{A negative claim needs a control}
  \fid{mutation}
  A list of statements the system fails to prove is worthless alone: a broken
  test looks exactly like a sound refusal. So each entry names the guard it
  watches, and we delete that guard on purpose.
  \codefile[\tiny]{mutation-table.txt}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item \Nmut{} guards, \Nmut{} predictions, checked by a command
          (\code{./mutation-check}) in about a second per mutant.
    \item It also demonstrates live, if the occasion allows: break a guard, one
          line goes red, put it back.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The best thing in the file}
  \fid{mutation-anecdote}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Changing \code{k > 0} to \code{k >= 0} in the linear-arithmetic
          contradiction test --- \emph{one character} --- left all entries
          green, and proves
          \[ a \le b,\ b \le a \ \vdash\ a < b. \]
    \item No entry could see it, because no entry's query ever formed a
          constant constraint.
    \item Two of the five mutations found the tests themselves to be vacuous on
          the first run.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \begin{center}
    \emph{The instrument for measuring whether we would notice a bug is how we
    found the bug we would not have noticed.}
  \end{center}
  \vfill
  \small Caveats to keep in the mouth: this covers the \Nmut{} guards mutated,
  not ``soundness bugs''; and the mutants replace whole procedures.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What would let more be claimed}
  \fid{soundness-roadmap}
  In increasing order of cost:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Certificate-producing oracles.} \code{ineq} already computes
          a Farkas certificate; emit it and check it.
    \item \textbf{A replay checker} (the de Bruijn criterion): a second, much
          smaller program that re-checks every stored derivation against the
          rule schemas alone --- no tactics, no macetes, no oracles. First
          question to settle: is the deduction graph retained after
          \code{qed}?
    \item \textbf{A kernel walkthrough document}: \Nkernel{} rules, each with
          the side condition it enforces. Writing it is how rule 51 gets found,
          and the \Nunexer{} unexercised rules are the place to start.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}
