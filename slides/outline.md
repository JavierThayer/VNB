## Part 0. Two disclosures (2 frames)

**F1. Who wrote these slides.**
The Beamer source was produced by an LLM (the Assistant), as was every line of the
prover. Not "assisted by": written by. The git history is the receipt -- every commit
co-authored.

**F2. Who is responsible for them.**
Direction, tone, mathematical content and editorial control are the speaker's. The
Assistant proposes; the speaker disposes. A deck about an experiment in LLM
authorship, written by anything other than the LLM, would be a worse deck and a
dishonest one.

## Part 1. The experiment (4 frames)

**F3. This is a project in progress.**
Not a released system. The talk reports a state, including what is unfinished and
what is broken. Numbers quoted are as of the day of the talk.

**F4. Motivation, stated as an opinion.**
LLMs are now applied to mathematics. In the speaker's opinion those applications are
mostly obscure -- flagged as personal opinion, and open for argument during the talk.
So the question asked here is a different one: not "can an LLM do mathematics", but
**can an LLM build the instrument** -- a proof checker.

**F5. Why a proof checker is the right test object.**
Its output is checkable without trusting the author's fluency: a proof passes the
kernel or it does not. Failure modes are visible and diagnosable, which is exactly
what "LLM writes a convincing English proof" lacks. The bet is on the artifact being
inspectable, not on the model being right.

**F6. Lineage: IMPS.**
Not built from nothing. The design is inspired by IMPS -- the speaker's own prior
work, so: frame it, do not explain it. What carried over: macetes, the deduction
graph, partial equality/definedness. What did not, and why.

## Part 2. VNB: the logic (5 frames)

**F7. Why von Neumann-Bernays.**
Classes are first-class objects of discourse, so "the class of groups" is sayable
without a metalanguage. Sets are the classes that can be members. What this buys in
practice for a structure library.

**F8. Equality is partial.**
`=` is partial; `t = t` asserts that `t` is defined. The single largest departure
from what an audience expecting HOL will assume. Worked micro-example.

**F9. The language.**
Terms and wffs; guarded quantification `forall([x in A], ...)`; `IOTA` definite
description; lambda carrying its domain (and therefore defined only there).

**F10. The kernel.**
~50 primitive inferences (`primitive-inferences.scm`); nothing else may assert
anything. Everything above -- tactics, macetes, drivers -- is untrusted and must
reduce to those.

**F11. The deduction graph.**
A proof is a navigation of a graph of sequent nodes; open leaves are the obligations;
`qed` when none remain. This is the object the whole user interface is about.

## Part 3. Structures (5 frames)

**F12. What a structure is here.**
A tuple plus a predicate. `declare-structure`, accessors with one name per slot
(`CARR`, `MUL`, ...), and the case-folding discipline that comes with it.

**F13. Ring, end to end.**
`def-structure-from-clauses` -> `IS-RING` -> the projected laws, on one frame, as
actual source.

**F14. The hierarchy.**
How "a Euclidean ring is a ring" is recorded and used
(`register-definitional-structure!`, the parent chain), and what the checker does
with it during a proof.

**F15. The operator table.**
One table keyed by head symbol, populated at definition time, read by the English
verbalizer, the documentation generator and the glossary. The point: notation is
declared once, next to the definition, not re-implemented per output format.
(Honest footnote: the TeX renderer does not read it yet.)

**F16. Design in progress: `FUBA-CLASS`.**
Candidate, not committed. Each structure `FUBA` would come with a class
`FUBA-CLASS` alongside `IS-FUBA`, so that
`forall([x], IS-FUBA(x) => BLAH)` may equivalently be written
`forall([x in FUBA-CLASS], BLAH)`. Nothing existing changes. Included as an example
of what "in progress" means concretely.
