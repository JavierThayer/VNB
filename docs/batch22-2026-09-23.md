## The four files (28 theorems)

**22-A, `theorem-library/regulated-step-approx.scm` (11).** Dieudonne (7.6.1), necessity:
`regulated-on-is-step-limit` -- a function regulated on [a, b] is the uniform limit on [a, b] of a
sequence of step functions; `regulated-on-step-approx` is the one-epsilon form. The proof is the
creeping argument rather than the source's finite subcover: the set of x in [a, b] such that f is
within epsilon of a step function on [a, x] contains a, is closed under the right limit within
[a, b] at its supremum, and reaches b. Every partition it builds grows by one point at the right
end (`strict-seq-append-point`, `fun-append-constant-piece`, `step-pieces-append`), so no finite
set of reals is ever sorted, and Heine-Borel is not used.

**22-B, `theorem-library/primitive-uniform-limit.scm` (7).** Dieudonne (8.6.4) on [a, b] with
countable exceptional sets: `primitive-uniform-limit` -- if g_k is a primitive of f_k on [a, b],
the f_k converge uniformly on [a, b] to f, and g_k(a) = 0, then the g_k converge to a primitive of
f. The estimate is the mean value inequality with a countable exceptional set (Dieudonne 8.5.3,
already in the tree) applied to g_k - g_m restricted to [x, y] (`primitive-pair-lipschitz`); the
limit function and its derivative reuse the machinery of the everywhere-derivative version
(`antiderivable-uniform-limit`) through the clamped family. The exceptional set of the limit is
the set of interior points at which some g_k fails to have derivative f_k(t): a countable union
over NN, `countable-union-nn`, proven from `nn-flatten` (the Cantor pairing of batch 20) with one
enumeration per index chosen by CHOICE. The source's un-normalised form (only g_n(x_0) converging)
is not stated; it follows from this one by subtracting constants.

**22-C, `theorem-library/primitive-glue.scm` (7).** `primitive-glue`: primitives on [a, c] and
[c, b] that agree at c glue to a primitive on [a, b]. The glued g and f are GIVEN functions with
agreement clauses (never an IF-term), the integrand has to agree only on the open pieces (so its
values at a, c, b are free, which the step case needs), and c goes into the countable exceptional
set. Then `step-fn-has-primitive`, by induction on the number of pieces of the partition (the
partition function is total on NN, so "the same p with n - 1" is literally the same p), gluing the
affine primitive of the last piece (`primitive-of-constant-piece`) shifted to agree at the joint.
Also `primitive-integrand-interior-congruence` and the two CLAMP value laws.

**22-D, `theorem-library/regulated-has-primitive.scm` (3).** The assembly:
`regulated-on-has-primitive` -- `forall a, b in rr, f. is-regulated-on(f, a, b) implies forsome g.
is-primitive(g, f, a, b)`. From 22-A the step family; from 22-C a primitive of each, restricted to
[a, b]; `primitive-shift-const` normalises each at a; `primitive-family-normalised-choice` picks
one per index (the pattern of `witness-family-choice.scm`); 22-B gives the primitive of f. Three
probes, no obstacle.

## What was found

* `use-cases` on a disjunction that is not already in context cuts it and leaves the obligation
  as a SILENT open leaf (seen by 22-A and 22-C). Cite the disjunction first. The positional mode
  should report the leaf it leaves open; kit item.
* Local `let` names that differ only by case (`seqg` / `seqG`) are one variable and the second
  silently shadows the first; `case-fold-define-lint!` checks top-level defines only. A lint for
  local binders is wanted (cost 22-B two probes).
* `dk-apply!` errors at an antecedent not yet in context; 22-B's local `pul-chain!` detaches a
  whole implication chain, proving each missing antecedent with the first matching prover, and
  turned the eight-antecedent `uniform-witness-diff-at` into one form. Kit candidate.
* Replacing a `vnb-lambda` family by a fresh function symbol plus its value equation avoids every
  beta reduction under the sequence binder. A kit helper for that step (`dk-abstract!`) is wanted.
* Closing `L == R` by `subst` of the identity `L = R` when R contains L rewrites both sides;
  rewrite away the side that contains the other (22-D's `rhp-crs-close!`; 22-C's `pgl-crs-close!`
  has the latent defect).
* `have!` with no thunk on a conjunction that contains a numeral fails even when every conjunct
  is in context: the known `from-context!` defect (it tries `arith` before `ass`).
* `dk-peel!` peels through a conclusion that is itself a FORALL or IMPLIES; landing lists come
  back newest first; after `ew`, a typing leaf already in context is grounded silently, so a
  helper expecting two leaves gets one.
