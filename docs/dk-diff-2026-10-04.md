# `dk-diff!`: the derivative of an explicit complex expression (2026-10-04)

Both agents of the first October batch named the same obstacle (docs/batch-2026-10-03-week1.md,
gap item 6): the derivative bookkeeping of an explicit function such as `c + r exp(i t)` is a page of
driver per function, and `winding-circle-inside` was estimated at 300 to 400 lines for the same
reason. The user's order (2026-10-04): build the tactic, and do not touch the kernel. This page
records what was built, how it works, what it owes, and what it was tested on. The code is the last
section of `driver-kit.scm`; the checks are the block "dk-diff! (2026-10-04)" of `test-suite.scm`.

## 1. The surface

    (dk-diff! U LAM A [L])    land IS-DIFF-ON(CC-NORMED-FIELD, U, LAM, A, L*) in the focus context
                              and return that formula; LAM is (VNB-LAMBDA x U e); L* is L when
                              given, else the textbook derivative of e at A
    (dk-diff!)                the same, read off a focus goal IS-DIFF-ON(...), which is then closed
    (dk-holomorphic! U LAM)   close the focus goal HOLOMORPHIC-ON(U, LAM)

The grammar of `e` over the bound variable `x`: `x`; a term without `x`; `+` and `*` (binary); `-`
(binary and unary); `recip`; `power` with a numeral exponent; `CC-EXP`, `CC-SIN`, `CC-COS`,
`CC-LOG`; and `(g s)` for a symbol `g` with `IS-DIFF-ON(CC-NORMED-FIELD, V, g, _, _)` or
`HOLOMORPHIC-ON(V, g)` in context. Anything else is an error naming the subterm. The field is
CC-NORMED-FIELD; the general-K laws are cited at it, and the read-offs, the typing table and the
exp / log / recip laws are CC's. A second field would add a table, not a mechanism.

Preconditions: `(IN A U)` in context; `U` known open, as `IS-OPEN(NF-METRIC-SPACE CC-NORMED-FIELD, U)`
or `IS-OPEN(CC-MS, U)` or `HOLOMORPHIC-ON(U, _)` in context (`U = CC` needs nothing; it is proved
open from `carrier-is-open`).

## 2. Nothing is asserted; what cannot be derived is left open and named

The side conditions the laws need and no tactic can invent are the non-vanishing of a denominator
on `U`, the slit-plane guard of a logarithm's argument, and the membership of an opaque function's
argument in its domain. The plan (section 3) collects them in the user's form, as universals
`forall y in U. ...`; each is looked up in the context, and one that is not there is posted by `cut`
on the main branch, left OPEN, and announced:

    ;VNB warning: dk-diff!: OWED, left open for you: forall([dkdoy_ in u], not(dkdoy_ = 0))

The proof is not done until the user closes it. This is the show-the-work rule applied to a tactic:
the derivative is landed, the proof continues, and the obligation stands where it belongs, as a leaf
with the user's own hypotheses in its context. The suite's control pins it: `recip(w)` on an open
`u` with no non-vanishing hypothesis lands its derivative and leaves exactly one leaf, the universal
above, and `proof-done?` is false.

## 3. How it works

**The plan first** (as `type-term`). A pure pass over `e` builds a tree of nodes `var`, `const`,
`sum`, `product`, `chain`, `recip`, `opaque`; binary `-` becomes a sum with a `(-1) *` product,
unary `-` a `(-1) *` product, a power a right-nested product. The pass accumulates the obligations
of section 2. Nothing is run until the whole tree exists, so a subterm outside the grammar changes
nothing.

**The obligations** are posted next, on the main branch, so that every lane below finds them in
context. (A `cut` inside a `dk-have!` lane would leave the lane ungrounded.)

**The engine** drives the library's laws bottom-up over the tree: `diff-on-identity`,
`diff-on-const`, `diff-on-sum`, `diff-on-product`, `diff-on-chain` (with `cc-exp-deriv`,
`cc-sin-deriv`, `cc-cos-deriv`, `cc-log-deriv`, or the opaque function's own fact, obtained from
`HOLOMORPHIC-ON` through `holomorphic-on-diff` and a skolem), `diff-on-recip-cc`. The function term
and the derivative of every instance are read off the formula `dk-cite!` landed, never rebuilt (the
rule "copy a constructor call, never its printed reading"). The laws produce a canonical lambda `h`
over K's own operations, `(ADD K)`, `(MUL K)`, `(ZERO K)`, `(ONE K)`, with the sub-lambdas applied
inside, and a K-form derivative `L_K`.

**The transfer** to the user's `LAM` is `diff-on-transfer-ptwise-eq`, whose two antecedents are
proved on lanes: `LAM in FUN(U, CARR K)` by `lam-t` with the body typed, and the pointwise equation
`forall y in U. LAM(y) == h(y)`, by beta to a fixpoint, then the read-offs `cc-nf-add-apply` /
`cc-nf-mul-apply` and `slot` for zero and one, then three de-normalising identities proved once per
proof by `crs` on a lane and instantiated (`p + (-1) q = p - q`, `p + (-q) = p - q`, `(-1) p = -p`,
and `p * (p * p) = p^3` for the powers met), after which the two sides agree and `qrfl` closes; when
they still differ the equation goes to `crs` with every reciprocal named by `dk-name!`.

**The derivative in user form.** `L*` is computed symbolically alongside (the product rule as the
books write it, zeros and ones folded). It reaches the landed formula through the universal

    forall v. v = L_K  =>  IS-DIFF-ON(K, U, LAM, A, v)

proved on a lane (one `subst` of the fresh `v`) and instantiated at `L*` once `L* = L_K` is proved by
beta, the read-offs and `crs`. A direct `subst` of `L*` in the goal would also rewrite its occurrences
inside `LAM` (the `c` of `c * x`). When that equation cannot be closed the formula is landed with
`L_K` and a warning says so.

Every step is a surface tactic (`fact`, `cut`, `di`, `lam-t`, `lam-b`, `subst`, `slot`, `crs`,
`qrfl`, `ass`, `keep`, `prop`, `ew`); the page replays without new machinery and no kernel file is
touched.

## 4. Three facts about the kernel this work had to learn

1. **`lam-b` under a binder.** One pass contracts every redex present in the goal, licensed or not;
   an unlicensed one (its argument untyped in context) posts its typing as a leaf and is contracted
   anyway; but a redex sitting under a binder whose argument mentions that binder is left standing,
   unless the argument is the binder's own variable and the domains agree. A redex a contraction
   creates is left for the next pass. The tactic predicts the reduct with this rule to tell the main
   leaf from the owed typings after each pass, reads the owed K-form argument off to the surface
   operations, types it and closes it, then goes on. (`dk-lam-b!` errors on an owed typing the
   context does not hold, which is right for its callers and wrong here.)
2. **`subst` of `CC == CARR(CC-NORMED-FIELD)` rewrites every CC**, the lambda domains included, so the
   conversion between the two readings of a typing goes through universals with the term or the
   domain quantified (`forall v. v in CARR K => v in CC`, and the FUN form with the domain
   quantified), proved once on a lane and instantiated. The direction `CARR K -> CC` is safe, since
   no user term contains `CARR K`.
3. **A definedness leaf from an application.** Instantiating a law at `CC-EXP(f(a))` with the
   typing of that term not yet in context leaves the leaf `t = t` open (the LUTINS rule: an untyped
   application is not certified). The outer derivative of a chain is typed before the chain law is
   cited; a skolem derivative is a variable and needs nothing.

## 6. Limits, and what is next

* One field. The tables are CC's; RR would be a second table (`rr-nf-*` read-offs, `rr-exp`...).
* No quotient `/` (write `* recip`), no `power` with a symbolic exponent, no `conj` / `magnitude`
  (not holomorphic anyway), no user-defined lambda applied inside the body (the opaque case wants a
  symbol).
* The logarithm wants three facts in context or posts them: the slit membership of the argument on
  `U`, the slit guard on `U`, and the non-vanishing on `U` (the last is for the typing of `recip`;
  it follows from the guard, and a lemma would retire it).
* The de-normalisers cover what the engine itself introduces. A body written with `/` or with a
  nested unary minus inside a product is closed by the `crs` fallback with reciprocals named, which
  is slower and may decline.
* Not done: the local copies this subsumes (`alg-kops!` in analytic-log-laws.scm and
  cc-log-holomorphic.scm, `cex-stage-apply!`, `alg-stage-apply!`, the derivative lane of
  winding-number-laws.scm around `cex-exp-comb-deriv`) are left for the retirement pass; a
  `(derivative)` surface command recorded as itself is not needed, since the page records the steps.
