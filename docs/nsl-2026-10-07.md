## The vocabulary (the user confirms)

* `PK-SPACE`: an infiltrated space (`PK-INF`), a set `NAMES` of tokens, and `PUBKEY` from names to keys,
  INJECTIVE (Lemma 5.7, Proposition 5.14 and the regular case of 5.13 need it).
* `IS-INIT-STRAND(nsp, s, A, B, Na, Nb)` and `IS-RESP-STRAND(...)`: the paper's two traces as literals,
  `[[SENT, {Na A}_KB], [RECEIVED, {Na Nb B}_KA], [SENT, {Nb}_KB]]` and its complement, with `A, B` names,
  `Na, Nb` tokens that are NOT names and NOT keys. Names and nonces are not keys: in the user's model
  keys are tokens, and without this rule Proposition 5.2 is false (a nonce equal to K_A^-1 leaks it).

* No `UNIQUELY-ORIGINATING`: S-2's `UNIQUELY-ORIGINATES` says exactly that.
* `{N_b}_KB` is `ENCR(Nb, KB)`, a token under a key, as the paper has it.

## The paper's gaps, found by proving it

* **Proposition 5.13 is false as stated**, in two ways. Two responders can answer the same first
  message; the second one's node then holds Na but neither of the two terms the paper names, with no
  adversary involved. And if Na = Nb, the initiator's own third node is a counterexample. Proved with
  Na /= Nb added and the second disjunct widened to `{Na x B}_KA` for some token x. Proposition 5.14
  rests on it and so carries Na /= Nb; whether 5.14 holds without that hypothesis is open.
* **Lemma 2.8 does not apply to the set T** of Lemma 5.4's case S (T is not closed under equal
  content); the weaker form the argument needs is `bundle-minimal-sent-back`.
* **Lemma 5.11 misses the case where Nb is a name.**
* **C-height 3 is not needed for 5.13.**
* Three hand-waves are now theorems: "no regular node contains an encrypted term as a proper subterm"
  (`nsl-regular-encr-subterm`), the "g h" claim of case S (`nsl-regular-list-subterm`), and "no regular
  strand originates K_A^-1" (`nsl-regular-no-key`).
